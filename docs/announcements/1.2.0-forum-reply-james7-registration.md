## 何を言われたか

前回の返信で「Surface Splines をドキュメントに載せた」と書いたことへの補足と訂正。

- **Surface Splines は必須ではない。** 精度は上がるが、**位置合わせにかかる時間がかなり伸びる**
- **長い星の軌跡があるものを位置合わせできるかは分からない。** 試したことがない。いつ問題になるかも分からない
- StarAlignment には歪みの大きい候補星を受け入れるオプションがあるが、長い軌跡には足りないだろう

## 投稿前にやったこと

**ページに時間のコストが書いていなかった。** 「精度が上がる」としか書いておらず、読んだ人が引き換えを知らずに選ぶことになる。日英とも直した（[pixinsight-repository #10](https://github.com/ysmrastro/pixinsight-repository/pull/10)）。あわせて「必須ではない」と「長い軌跡は未検証」も明示した。

## なぜこう書いたか

**手元のデータは彼の質問に答えていない。それを最初に言う。** 4 時間 13 分のデータは 13 秒露出・24mm で、1 枚ごとの星はほぼ点像である。**長い露出ではなく長い夜**であり、彼が聞いているのは前者。ここを混ぜると、答えていないものを答えたことにしてしまう。

**そのうえで、失敗の場所は彼の役に立つ。** StarAlignment は 1045 枚中 718 枚を解き、327 枚で `Unable to find an initial linear transformation` を出した。**歪みモデルに到達する前に諦めている。**

### 下書きの誤りを 1 つ直した（実機で確認）

最初の下書きは「Surface Splines も、歪みの大きい候補星を受け入れるオプションも、そもそも到達されない」と書いていた。**後半が誤り。** `ImageRegistration-pxm.dylib` の UI 文字列を引いて確かめた。

- **`Registration model:`** は実在するドロップダウンで、選択肢は `Projective Transformation`（既定）/ `Rigid Transformation` / **`Two-Dimensional Surface Splines`**。PJSR では `distortionCorrection`（真偽値、既定 `false`）
- PixInsight 自身の説明: *"a linear model is always necessary to build an initial registration transformation before modeling distortions"* → **スプラインは線形変換の後**。327 枚には出番が無い。ここは合っていた
- しかし **`Maximum star distortion`（既定 0.6）は星検出のパラメータ**で、照合より前に効く。*"The coverage of a perfectly circular star is π/4 (about 0.8). Lower values denote elongated or irregular sources."* — **伸びた光源を明示的に扱うつまみであり、彼の推測は正しい場所を指している**

**彼のつまみは上流にあり、こちらのデータはそこに触れていない。** 訂正したうえで、彼の勘が当たっていることを書いた。

**参照フレームの選び直しが何を直して何を直さないかも書く。** 枚数の喪失は直る（中央参照にすると 3 枚とも 6 秒前後で解けた）が、欠損は 66% → 59% にしかならない。回転した分だけ重ならないという幾何であり、これが 1.2.0 で位置合わせをやめた理由そのものである。

```
[QUOTE="james7"]Surface Splines are NOT required to do the registration on non-tracked images, but IMO that option does provide better accuracy. The downside for Surface Splines is that the registration takes a lot longer.[/QUOTE]

Thank you - the page had it as "more accurate" and said nothing about the cost, which would have had people choosing it without knowing what they were paying for it. It now says not required, and says the registration takes a great deal longer. That is the third change this thread has made to that page.

On long star trails I am no better off than you. My fixed-tripod set is 13-second exposures at 24mm, so the stars are close to round in any single frame. It is a long night rather than a long exposure - four hours and thirteen minutes, about 63 degrees of field rotation - so it does not answer the question you are actually asking.

What it does say may still be worth something to you, because the failure is not where I would have guessed. StarAlignment solved 718 frames of 1045 and failed on 327 with [I]Unable to find an initial linear transformation[/I]. The failures are not scattered: the first four hundred frames of the night failed at a rate of one hundred per cent, and nothing after frame 1370 failed at all. That is distance from the reference frame, not bad luck.

[B]It gave up before the distortion model came into it at all.[/B] StarAlignment's own note on the registration model says a linear model is always necessary first, to build an initial transformation before any distortion is modelled - so on those 327 frames the splines never got the chance to matter, whatever they might have been worth.

The star acceptance side is a different matter, and I think you were pointing at the right knob there. Maximum star distortion sits in star detection, before any of the matching, and it is explicitly about elongated sources - a perfectly round star scores about 0.8 and the default cut is 0.6. So it is upstream of where my frames gave up, which means my data cannot tell you whether opening it up would carry a real trail. Mine were not trailed. They were simply a long way from the reference.

Choosing a reference in the middle of the night does fix the frame loss - the three frames I tested then solved in about six seconds each - but it does not fix what is left behind. The worst empty area only comes down from 66% to 59%, because a field that has rotated away from its reference does not overlap it, and no setting changes that.

Which is why 1.2.0 stopped trying for a night that long. The composite is built on one of the unregistered frames instead and nothing is aligned at any stage. Thirty minutes is a different proposition, as you found.
```
