## What he answered

The previous reply asked three things and committed us to acting on the
answers. All three came back.

- **StarAlignment.** Nothing special was needed - his exposures were short
  enough that the stars are round - but *"given the field rotation it does
  align more accurately if you use Surface Splines as the registration
  model"*.
- **Which options removed the edge-of-frame defects.** Not detection options:
  the **Strict cutoff**. Several hundred candidates down to under ten, with
  both meteors inside the ten.
- **The rejection map.** He could not say whether the second meteor would have
  shown up in it, and *"there were a LOT of satellite trails in the rejection
  map and a good number of 'hot' pixels"*.

He also reports that the composite worked, and asks whether an integrated
coverage mask from ImageRegistration is what `Mask > Image` takes.

## What was done before replying

Both promises were kept first, as with 1.1.0.

1. **Surface Splines is on the documentation page**, credited to him.
2. **The rejection-map section carries his caveat now.** We had put his idea up
   as a way to get an independent list, which is more than he claimed for it -
   he had reported seeing one streak, not that the map was a clean list. It now
   says the map carries satellite trails and hot pixels, and is one more list
   to check against rather than the ground truth about what was missed.

## Why the reply says what it says

**The Strict answer is the one that mattered and it went the other way.** The
previous reply said: if the honest answer is that you had to work around it,
then the defaults are wrong and I would rather fix them. The answer was that he
used the one control that exists for this and it worked. So the defaults stay,
and that has to be said plainly rather than quietly.

**His numbers are the first evidence about the cutoff from outside our own
data.** Strict is 0.20, read off a single night - ours. A threshold read off one
night is fitted to that night, and we have said so in the documentation. Several
hundred down to under ten with both meteors surviving is that number holding on
somebody else's sky, at a different focal length, with a different camera. Two
nights is not many. It is twice one.

**He is about to walk into the mask bug.** He says he may go back and feed a
coverage mask into `Mask > Image`. On 1.1.0 a mask with any real depth drew a
straight line along its own boundary in almost every frame - 1041 of 1300
candidates on the fixed-tripod sequence in hand - and, worse, that line raised
the detection threshold and hid real candidates. Telling him after he spent the
evening would be useless.

**And he should not do it at all.** The area alignment leaves empty is already
excluded per frame without being told. Painting over it by hand only risks
covering something: on our own night three confirmed meteors sit four pixels
from the frame edge, and one of those was found by eye before the script
existed. This is already on the documentation page; it is worth saying to his
face because he is about to do it.

It is - black is excluded, so a coverage mask goes in the right way round with no inverting. But [B]I would not do it[/B], and I would rather say so than let you spend an evening on it.

The empty area the alignment leaves around each frame is already kept out of detection. The script works out where a frame has no data and excludes it, frame by frame, without being told. Painting over it by hand only adds the chance of covering something real: on my own night three of the confirmed meteors sit four pixels from the frame edge, and one of those I had found with my own eyes before any of this existed. A margin wide enough to feel safe would have taken it.

There is a second reason to wait. [B]1.2.0 is on the repository now[/B], and it fixes something you would have run straight into. A mask with any real depth was drawing a straight line along its own boundary in almost every frame - on the fixed-tripod sequence I have been testing with, 1041 of 1300 candidates were that one line. It came from the way the background is estimated: the covered area was being treated as though it were sky at zero, which pulled the estimate down just above the boundary and left the sky standing above it. The line was also raising the detection threshold and hiding real candidates, which is the part that actually costs you something.

The same release is mostly about the case yours is not. A fixed tripod can now be run without registering anything at all: detection reads the debayered frames and the composite is built on one of them, so the meteors land where they were against the landscape. Thirty minutes registers perfectly well, as you found, so this changes nothing for you. Four hours does not - on a sequence I have here StarAlignment lost 327 frames of 1045 and left an average of 36% of each survivor empty, and once you align the sky to integrate it, the landscape in the master comes out as an arc.
