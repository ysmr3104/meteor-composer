## What james7 reported

The first run of this script on anyone else's data.

- 400+ frames, **fixed tripod**, 50 mm on full frame, ~30 minutes, very short exposures, no shower
- He chose the sequence because he had already seen a meteor-like streak in **PixInsight's pixel rejection map** from integrating all the images. The script proposed that same object, and he confirmed it
- 2 meteors found. Several hundred candidates, ~10 flagged as likely meteors; the rest satellites and edge-of-frame defects, "which could be easily excluded by toggling some of the detection options"
- "Overall, I think a successful use of your new script."

## Why the reply says what it says

**The rejection map is the substance.** Every number quoted so far compares the
script against my own eyes on my own night. This is an object identified before
the script ran, by a method with nothing to do with it. It is also a way out of
a problem in how the help was asked for: "tell me about a meteor that never
showed up as a candidate" only works if you already know what you missed. The
rejection map does not have that problem, so the reply turns it into a countable
request.

**A prediction became a measurement.** Post-183331 said the shape and length
tests should reject slightly trailed stars and was explicit that this was a
guess. His false candidates were satellites and edge defects, not stars. The
reply says the guess held and states the limit: short exposures are the easy
case.

**The workaround is not accepted as the answer.** He got rid of the edge defects
by toggling options. That is his workaround, not evidence that the defaults are
right — see CLAUDE.md, "操作者の回避策を原因の証拠に使ってはいけない". So the
reply asks what he switched on and how much it removed.

**His case is harder than mine for a measurable reason.** A field that drifts
for thirty minutes leaves a far larger empty area than my tracked frames, where
it was 0.24% to 0.42%. That empty area is also what 1.1.0 fixes in the fit, so
he is told before he composites anything.

Every number I have quoted so far for how many meteors it finds compares the script against my own eyes on my own data. This is the first one that does not.

```
It also fixes a problem with what I asked for. "Tell me about a meteor that never showed up as a candidate" only works if you already know what you missed, which is the one thing nobody knows. The rejection map does not have that problem: it is a separate list, made before the script ran. So if you still have that integration — [B]how many meteor-like streaks are in the rejection map, and how many of them turned up in the candidate list?[/B] That would be the first count that does not come from my own night, and it would answer your own question about how many might have been recorded. I will put the idea on the documentation page either way, because I would not have thought of it.

Two things your run settles that I could not settle myself.

[LIST]
[*]Star trails. I said the shape and length tests should throw out slightly trailed stars rather than report them — a candidate has to be at least six times longer than it is wide, and at least 80 pixels long — and I was careful to call that a guess rather than something I had measured. Your false candidates were satellites and edge-of-frame defects, not stars, so the guess held. The honest limit is that very short exposures are the easy case; longer exposures with longer star trails are still untried.
[*]Alignment. I said StarAlignment on a drifting fixed-tripod field would be the step most likely to stop you. It did not. Thirty minutes is several degrees of rotation, and how you got 400+ frames to solve would help the next person more than anything I can write. If you remember what you did, I would like to put it on the page.
[/LIST]

The edge-of-frame defects are what I want to follow up on. There is something for them already: alignment leaves an empty area around the edge of every frame, and a candidate lying along the border of that empty area has its score cut hard. [B]It never throws one away, on purpose[/B] — on my night three of the confirmed meteors sit 4 pixels from the frame edge, and one of those I had found with my own eyes before the script existed. But your case is harder than mine for a simple reason. A field that drifts for thirty minutes leaves a far larger empty area than my tracked frames did, where it was 0.2 to 0.4% of the frame, and its border moves from frame to frame. So: which options did you switch on, and roughly how many of the several hundred candidates went away when you did? If the honest answer is that you had to work around it, then the defaults are wrong, and I would rather fix them than leave you toggling.

One thing before you composite anything. [B]1.1.0 is on the repository now[/B], and it fixes a mistake in how a frame is matched to the master. That empty area was being included in the match — the frame reads zero there and the master does not — so the more of the frame that was empty, the more wrong the brightness match came out. On my tracked frames that is a fraction of a per cent. On yours it will not be. Detection and screening are unaffected, so nothing you have already done is wasted.

Two meteors in thirty minutes on a night with no shower sounds right to me, and thank you for saying so plainly.
```
